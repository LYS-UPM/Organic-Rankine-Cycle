# Organic Rankine Cycle
This project explores the thermodynamic potential of Organic Rankine Cycle (ORC) systems in renewable energy applications. The core objective is to identify the optimal working fluid that maximizes net power output while minimizing environmental impact (Global Warming Potential - GWP).

# Methodology & Computational Approach
Simulation Engine: Developed a Python-based thermodynamic model to simulate cycle efficiencies under varying heat source conditions.

Parametric Study: Analyzed the impact of evaporator temperature scaling (80°C to 150°C) on overall thermal efficiency.

Fluid Candidates: Evaluated a baseline legacy fluid (R245fa) against next-generation, low-GWP alternatives (R1233zd(E) and Isobutane).

# Key Engineering Insights
The data visualization reveals a critical thermodynamic behavioral shift: while Isobutane suffers from efficiency bottlenecks at higher temperatures, the low-GWP fluid R1233zd(E) successfully surpasses the legacy R245fa. This proves that sustainable, eco-friendly refrigerants can drive the future of high-efficiency waste heat recovery systems.
